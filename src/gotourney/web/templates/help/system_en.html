    The tournament system

    Instead of McMahon points, every player has a tournament rating (TGoR). It starts at the player's EGD rating and changes after every round with the EGD formula – only faster. Whoever has the highest TGoR at the end wins.

    In short

    1. Everyone starts with their EGD rating (GoR){% if cfg.rating.top_bar is not none %}, but at most {{ "%g"|format(cfg.rating.top_bar) }} ({{ rank_of(cfg.rating.top_bar) }}){% endif %}.
    2. In every round you play someone with a rating as close as possible to yours – never the same opponent twice.
    3. Games are played with {% if cfg.handicap.reduction %}handicap (full handicap minus {{ cfg.handicap.reduction }}){% else %}full handicap{% endif %}, so every game is a fair fight.
    4. After the round, a win raises your rating and a loss lowers it – more if the result was a surprise.
    5. The player with the highest rating after the last round wins the tournament.

    The starting rating

    Your starting TGoR is your current EGD rating (GoR) on the day of registration. Players without an EGD rating start at the value of their rank: 1k = 2000, 1d = 2100, and 100 points per rank (5k = 1600, 10k = 1100, 3d = 2300). The tournament director may correct a starting rating that is clearly out of date – for example of a player who has not played a rated tournament for years.

    {% if cfg.rating.top_bar is not none %}

    The top bar

    Starting ratings are capped at the top bar of {{ "%g"|format(cfg.rating.top_bar) }} ({{ rank_of(cfg.rating.top_bar) }}). All stronger players start exactly at the bar. Without it, the strongest player would start with such a lead that the tournament would be decided before the first game. With the bar, the top players start level and the title is decided on the board.

    For the tie-break (see winner), the real starting GoR is still used.

    After every round

    When all results of a round are in, every player's TGoR is recalculated with the formula of the EGD rating system (version 2021). The idea: each game has an expected result from the two ratings and the handicap. If you do better than expected, your rating goes up; if you do worse, it goes down.

    expected result SE = 1 / (1 + e^(β(opponent) − β(you))) β(r) = −7 · ln(3300 − r − h) new rating r' = r + k · con · (S − SE) con = ((3300 − r) / 200)^1.6
    • r = rating before the round, S = 1 for a win, ½ for jigo, 0 for a loss.
    • h = handicap bonus of the player taking black: 0 in an even game, otherwise 100 · (stones − 0.5).

    Two differences to the EGD:

    • The k-factor ({{ "%g"|format(cfg.rating.k_factor) }}) multiplies every change. In the EGD it is 1. With a bigger k-factor the ratings move fast enough to separate the players within a few rounds.
    • The EGD's small bonus against deflation is not used.

    All games of a round are rated from the ratings before the round, so the order in which results come in does not matter. con is larger for lower ratings: the ratings of weaker players move more, just like in the EGD.

    Pairing

    Every round is paired so that the rating differences are as small as possible – over the whole field at once (with the “blossom” algorithm), not board by board from the top. Two players never meet twice.

    To be exact, the program minimises the sum of the squared differences. Squaring makes one big mismatch worse than several small ones: two pairs with 50 points each are better than one pair with 0 and one with 100.

    Colours and boards

    • In a handicap game the stronger player takes white.
    • In an even game the colours are balanced: whoever had white more often gets black. If both had the same colours, the colour is drawn.
    • Board 1 is the pair with the highest rating, and so on downwards.

    Handicap and komi

    The handicap is one stone per full 100 points of rating difference (always rounded down, at most 9) {% if cfg.handicap.reduction %}, minus {{ cfg.handicap.reduction }}{% endif %}. Players may agree on another handicap before the game; the handicap that was actually played is used for the rating.

    Handicap 1 means: no stones, Black plays first, komi {{ "%g"|format(cfg.handicap.komi_handicap) }}.

    The handicap evens out most of the difference, so the games are close. Because it is rounded down, the stronger player is still slightly favoured – the rating formula knows this (each stone counts as 100 points, the first one as 50) and rewards an upset accordingly.

    Odd number of players

    If an odd number of players is registered for a round, one joker sits out. Jokers are players who agreed to this at registration – often local players or helpers. Of the jokers, the one who has played the most games sits out; if that is a tie, the program draws. A player who sits out keeps their rating for that round.

    Jigo, forfeits, late players

    • Jigo (a draw) counts as half a win for both players.
    • Forfeits (a game not played) are not rated: the ratings of both players do not change, and the game does not count as a win. The two players are still treated as having met, so they are not paired again.
    • Players who miss a round keep their rating; they can play again in the next round.
    • Late players can be paired among each other after a round has started.

    Winner and prizes

    The winner is the player with the highest TGoR after the last round. If two players have the same rating, the one with the lower starting GoR ranks higher – they achieved more. If that is equal too, the draw decides.

    Other prizes – most wins, the biggest rating gain, the best player of an age group – are set by the tournament director and shown in the award ceremony.

    Why this system?

    • Every game is close. You always play someone of your current strength, with handicap – no hopeless games in the first rounds as with McMahon bars.
    • Surprises count. A win against a stronger player (or with fewer stones) moves the rating more than an expected win.
    • No McMahon bar, no score groups. The rating itself shows who is doing well, and it is the familiar EGD rating, so players can read it.
    • Fair title. Thanks to the top bar the strongest players start level and play for the title among each other.
